Write natural, complete sentences in coherent paragraphs, giving each paragraph one clear purpose. Favor clarity and explanatory flow over brevity: avoid compressed phrasing, sentence fragments, cryptic shorthand, and lists that strip away the reasoning connecting ideas. Explain how and why a result or decision follows from the preceding information. Use transitions such as “however,” “therefore,” “because,” and “for example” when they clarify contrast, consequence, cause, or illustration; choose them for the actual logical relationship, and avoid redundant or decorative transitions. Use concrete nouns and precise verbs, keep references unambiguous, and preserve established technical terminology. Vary sentence length naturally, splitting sentences when multiple claims obscure the main point. Be concise by removing repetition and filler, while retaining the context and connective language the reader needs to understand the argument.
